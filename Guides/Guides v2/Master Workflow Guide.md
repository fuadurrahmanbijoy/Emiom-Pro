# Master Workflow Guide: From Course File to Finished Book

*How the guides fit together, who decides what, and the order of work. Read this first; it tells you when to open each of the other guides.*

---

## 1. The Set of Documents

| Document | It answers | Used when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Course-to-Book Guide** | *What* goes in the book, and in what order | Planning: turning the course file into chapters, sections, example plans, and practice plans |
| **Intellectual Guide** | *How* to teach each idea (voice, spine, length, examples, numbers, referencing, chapter shape) | Writing every chapter |
| **Beginner Guide** | *How much extra care* an absolute beginner needs (language, definitions, pace, sequencing) | Writing every chapter, layered on top of the Intellectual Guide |
| **Visual Guide** | *How the book looks* (page, type, headings, side column, cover, print) | Laying out and proofing; also shapes how text is written (side notes, footnotes) |
| **Specimen spread** | A real page showing the visual guide in use | Whenever the layout needs checking |
| **This guide** | The order of work and the rules for conflicts | Always |

**The course file** (objectives, topics, past questions) is **private input**. It is used only in planning and checking, and it never appears in the book.

---

## 2. Who Decides What

When guides seem to disagree, use this order.

1. **Course-to-Book Guide decides the content and order.** What is covered, how topics are grouped, where examples and practice go.
2. **Intellectual Guide decides the method of teaching.** Structure of a concept, register, length tiers, chapter shape.
3. **Beginner Guide adjusts pace and language.** Where it asks for shorter sentences, slower pace, or simpler definitions, it overrides the Intellectual Guide for that passage.
4. **Visual Guide decides presentation.** Writing must fit what the page can carry (for instance, every paragraph should be summarisable in one line for the side column).
5. **The author decides anything left open**, and the answer is recorded in the decision log (section 6).

**Two standing rules that override everything**

- The back story (objectives, topics, past questions, marks, frequency) never appears in the book.
- Question frequency and marks never change how large or emphasised a topic is. They affect only examples and end-of-chapter practice.

## 3. Adapting to the Type of Course

The Intellectual Guide's rules for calculations apply **where a course has calculations**. For a descriptive course (such as one on the nature of business, forms of ownership, trade, and institutions):

- Replace "worked example" with a **worked case**: Situation, Question, Reasoning, Conclusion, Check. It applies an idea to a situation; it is not a model exam answer.
- Replace the change-the-numbers ladder with a change-the-**situation** ladder: the same idea in a familiar case, then a reshaped case, then a new setting, then a case that combines earlier ideas.
- Present distinctions (for example, between two forms of organisation) in a short **comparison table**, introduced in words first.
- Use the running business and verified historical cases as the source of scenarios.

The book never includes model exam answers or a guide on writing them. Its job is to build understanding complete enough that a reader can answer.

## 4. The Workflow, Stage by Stage

### Stage 0: Set up once

1. Choose the book title, spelling convention (British or American), default country, currency, and date format.
2. Decide the running business (type and starting facts).
3. Open the working files (section 6).
4. Fix the layout using the specimen spread.

### Stage 1: Inventory the course file

Follow **Course-to-Book steps 1-4**: list objectives, topics, and questions; flatten topics; profile the questions; map questions to topics; log unmapped questions and topics with no questions.

### Stage 2: Analyse

Follow **steps 5-6**: break topics into concepts, draw the dependency map, and decide what foundation material a beginner needs first.

### Stage 3: Blueprint

Follow **steps 7-9**: form chapters and sections by teaching logic, order by dependency, handle overlaps, and fill in one blueprint entry per chapter.

> **Checkpoint 1.** The author reviews the blueprint before any drafting begins: chapter list, order, sections, and foundation material.

### Stage 4: Prepare each chapter

For the chapter about to be written:

1. Read its blueprint entry.
2. Extend the case file with this chapter's step in the running business, carrying forward all earlier figures.
3. Choose and verify the historical case.
4. Plan the example ladder and the end-of-chapter practice (**step 10**).
5. Add the chapter's new terms to the term bank.

### Stage 5: Draft the chapter

Write in this order:

1. **The opening puzzle** (half to one page).
2. **The sections.** For each concept:
   - Choose the approach by kind of topic (Intellectual Guide, section 3).
   - Follow the layered spine: idea, example, rule, exceptions.
   - Choose the tier for length by importance, not frequency; three lines is the minimum.
   - Apply the Beginner Guide: short sentences for new material, every term defined in simple English in bold at first use, standard or slow pace as the idea demands.
   - Write the side-column line for each paragraph, and key-term notes where a term appears.
3. **The worked case** from the running business.
4. **The closing pack**, in order: summary, think it through, where people go wrong, in history, sources only for defining topics.
5. **The end-of-chapter practice** from the plan.

### Stage 6: Check the chapter

Run all of these before calling a chapter done:

- Intellectual Guide checklist (section 10).
- Beginner Guide checklist (section 10).
- **Silent coverage check** (Course-to-Book step 11): every past question tied to this chapter is answerable from it.
- **Clean-out scan** (step 8): no objective labels, exam years, marks, frequency remarks, or copied questions.
- **Fact check:** every date, name, figure, and legal point verified against a reliable source.
- **Continuity check:** terms, case-file figures, and cross-references agree with the working files.

> **Checkpoint 2.** After the first chapter is finished, the author reviews it fully and confirms that the voice, pace, and level are right. Adjustments are made to the guides themselves before writing the rest.

### Stage 7: Assemble

Write front matter ("To the Reader" note, preface, contents) and back matter (glossary from the term bank, further reading, index). Collect review questions in their place.

### Stage 8: Lay out and proof

1. Build the pages to the Visual Guide and compare them with the specimen.
2. Print a **dummy** of 8-16 pages on the intended paper.
3. Test-read with a few real beginners and note where they slow down or get lost.
4. Correct the text and the guides.

### Stage 9: Final pass and print

Repeat the coverage check and the clean-out scan on the whole book. Order a proof from the printer before the print run.

## 5. Working with a Writing Assistant

If an assistant helps with drafting, give it the same inputs a human writer would need, and keep each session to **one chapter**.

**At the start of a session, supply:**

1. The Intellectual, Beginner, and Visual guides.
2. This guide.
3. The chapter's blueprint entry.
4. The current term bank, case file, and decision log.
5. A line stating the rules that override everything (section 2).

**Do not supply** the raw course file with a request to "write the book." Planning and drafting are separate jobs.

**Prompt template**

> Write Chapter \[number\], "\[working title\]". Follow the attached guides and the blueprint entry below. Keep to the terms in the term bank and the facts in the case file. Do not refer to objectives, topics, exams, marks, or past papers. Begin with the opening puzzle. Mark every new term in bold at first use. Write a side-column line for every paragraph. Finish with the closing pack and the practice set from the plan. After the draft, list every new term, every new case-file fact, every fact needing verification, and any cross-reference you created.

The assistant's closing list feeds the working files, so nothing is lost between sessions.

## 6. Working Files

Keep these separate from the manuscript. None is printed.

| File | Holds | Updated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inventory** | Objectives, topics, questions as found | Stage 1 |
| **Question profile** | Types, skills, frequencies, question families | Stage 1 |
| **Concept map** | Concepts, prerequisites, dependency order | Stage 2 |
| **Chapter blueprint** | One entry per chapter | Stage 3; revised as needed |
| **Coverage matrix** | Each question, topic, and objective traced to the sections covering it | Stages 3 and 6 |
| **Term bank** | Each term, chosen word, plain definition, chapter of first use, "Word in use" flag | Every chapter |
| **Case file** | The running business: timeline, figures, decisions | Every chapter |
| **Fact log** | Each verified fact with its source | Every chapter |
| **Decision log** | Every choice made, added topic, merged topic, and open question | Continuously |

A sensible layout is one folder for guides, one for private planning files (including the course file), one for chapter drafts, one for layout, and one for final output.
